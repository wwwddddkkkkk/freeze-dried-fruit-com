Freeze-dried fruit is highly sensitive to moisture. Once exposed to humid air, the fruit can begin absorbing water and lose its crisp texture. This is why packaging matters so much. Barrier films, seals, desiccants, oxygen absorbers, and pouch size can all affect how long the product stays crunchy.

## What "low moisture" can and cannot tell you

Moisture content reports how much water is in a sample. It is useful for specifying a finished product, but it does not by itself establish crunch, shelf life, or food safety. A meaningful number needs its basis, method, sampling point, and acceptance range. Those details can differ by fruit, cut, formulation, and package.

Water activity adds a different measurement: it describes the availability of water under the test conditions. FDA notes that water activity changes with temperature and that measurement requires temperature control. Neither a moisture percentage nor an aw result can replace product-specific package and storage validation.

For a supplier specification, distinguish [moisture content from water activity](/articles/water-activity-vs-moisture-content/). A texture observation, a moisture result, and an aw result answer different parts of the quality question.

## Keep texture, stability, and safety separate

Crunch is a sensory and handling outcome. Water activity is also used in food-safety and stability work, but a single aw threshold is not a universal promise that a commercial fruit product is safe, shelf-stable, or still crisp. The full product, its processing controls, package integrity, storage conditions, and validation matter. Freeze-drying also does not itself eliminate microorganisms, so a supplier's process controls remain relevant.

## Where moisture sneaks in

- A bag seal that wasn't fully closed at the factory
- A pouch whose barrier and seal are not suited to the product's storage conditions
- Headspace too large, allowing moist air with each open
- Storage in a humid kitchen or warm shelf
- Resealing too slowly after each serving

## What buyers can ask suppliers

Ask for the target moisture and water activity, the method and temperature used for aw testing, the packaging film and seal specification, the desiccant or oxygen-absorber strategy, and shelf-life testing data. The answer tells you whether the supplier treats moisture as a casual concern or as a controlled specification.
